## What works

- **Clocks** (T4.1, T4.2): a `Signal` is one clock's reading on one object at a moment. It's *good*, *watch* or
  *act*, with its reason as data and the date it next changes, and it's always an **Estimate**. Each library clock is
  one pure function: `saveBattery`, `codeExpiry`, `drinkingWindow`, `serviceRisk`, `mediaRot`/`discRot` and the
  opt-in `unfinished`. D-56 records the thresholds.
  - **Type files:** disc rot is held back on Saturn, PlayStation and music's CDs (PD-018). Video games gain
    `codeExpires`. `unfinished` is opt-in (D-57).
  - **Media clocks** start at the first backup, and **Make a backup** is their remedy (D-58).
- **Status** (T4.3): `ObjectStatus.derive` and `ShelfSignals`. What the clocks read (last service, last backup,
  playing since, dismissals) lives on the object as `signalState`, kept by `apply`. So a whole shelf's signals need no
  history, and a replay rebuilds them (D-59).
- **Dismissals** (T4.4): `dismissSignal` re-reads the clock and logs the reading with the parameters in force (risk
  R2). A dismissal holds until the reading gets more urgent or there's new evidence for that clock alone (D-60). There
  is no new table, so nothing changes for CloudKit sync.
- **The object's status slot** (T4.5): one chip, then each signal's reason under **ESTIMATE**, with **Dismiss**.
  Remedies sit in the actions: Log a battery swap, Make a backup, Log a service.
- **The signal feed** (T4.6): the Today strip's *need attention* line opens it (`Route.signals`), *act* before
  *watch*. Each row opens its object.
- **Status filters** (T4.7): pills in Collection and a chip in Find, over the derived status.
- **Notifications** (T4.8): `NotificationsClient`, and a card on the Shelf home that offers them once, in plain
  words, when a clock first has something to read. With the offer accepted:
  - each signal's next change is scheduled at 10:00 on its day;
  - a dismissed signal is scheduled for the day it would show again;
  - each time the Shelf appears, the schedule is replaced from the signals then (D-61).
- **Seed** (T4.9): 105 objects in eight collections, up from 16. Exactly one needs attention, Tetris, a Game Boy
  cartridge whose battery is due. The other old carts have swaps logged, Star Fox 64 is dismissed, and a download
  code reads *good*.

## Review

A separate review agent read T4.1 to T4.9 against the plan, F15 and D-56 to D-61. Every finding is fixed.

1. **`plan` trusted the caller's signal.** A stale or invented reading could be dismissed, and logged with a status
   the clock never read. `plan` now re-reads the clock at the command's moment.
2. **New evidence lifted every dismissal.** It now lifts only that clock's: each clock names the values and events
   it reads.
3. **The Today count and the feed disagreed** for an overdue object with its own signal. It now counts on both lines.
4. **A status filter could get stuck** once its last object changed status. The chosen status stays offered.
5. **A dismissed signal got no notification** on the day it shows again. Now it does.
6. **A change due later today could be missed.** It now fires a minute later instead.

Tests added for each one: refusals of a wrong or absent reading, one clock's evidence leaving another's dismissal,
rescheduling that actually moves a notification, a dismissal through the store's rebuild. The review also caught that
journey 5 expected "1 need attention" where English says "1 needs attention".

Answered in the decisions rather than code:
- the offer now waits for a clock with something to read (D-61);
- the Lent chip keeps its *watch* label while the filters read the derived status (D-59).

Journey 5 found one more, in the app rather than the review: the *need attention* line only answered a tap on its
words or its arrow, not on the space between. The line and the feed's rows now take a tap anywhere.

Nits taken: reason dates follow the screen's locale, the library's comment names the right function, and the unused
"Made in…" string is gone.

## Decisions

M4 added D-56 to D-61:
- **D-56:** the clocks' thresholds;
- **D-57:** disc rot held back, code expiry, the unfinished game;
- **D-58:** a media check is a backup (your answer), starting at the first one;
- **D-59:** status, and what the clocks read;
- **D-60:** dismissals;
- **D-61:** clock notifications.

**For the owner:**
- **D-56:** every threshold is my reading, except the battery's 25–35 years, which is F15's. They're parameters in
  `ClockLibrary`, and dismissals are logged so they can be tuned.
- **D-58:** a tape has no signal until its first backup; **Make a backup** is offered until then. Counting from the
  year it was made flooded the feed.
- **D-61:** the offer is a card on the Shelf home rather than a system prompt.
