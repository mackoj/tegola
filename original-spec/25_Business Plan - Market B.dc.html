CLCTN · BUSINESS PLAN · MARKET B · ANDROID-FIRST · 19 SEP 2026
A different business, wearing the same app.
Market B is not market A with lower prices. The user is a different person, the storefront is a different app, the money moves on different rails, the app is found in a different place, and the cost base has to be structurally lower. Five differences — so it earns a plan of its own rather than a discount column in the existing one.
Read alongside the Business Plan (market A) and Business Plan · Android. Numbers illustrative and deliberately conservative, as in the original.
Nothing is sold once. Everything is sold small and often — and no user who has never paid may cost you a cent to serve.
Market A's principle was sell once for what costs you nothing monthly. It doesn't transfer, because there is no "once" here — a single €25 decision is a barrier, while ₹49 a month is not a decision at all. The second half matters more: at this ARPU, one recurring cent per free user eats the business. Zero marginal cost isn't hygiene here, it's the whole model.

02 · THE STOREFRONT IS WHATSAPP, NOT YOUR APP
THE ONE PRODUCT DECISION THAT DECIDES THE REST
Selling happens in group chats. Your job is to make the message, not to host the sale.
In these markets the trade already has a venue and it is a WhatsApp group of 180 people who collect the same thing. Nobody is going to migrate that group into your marketplace, and the plan already concluded you shouldn't try — market C's whole lesson is that fighting off-platform completion is unwinnable.
So invert it. The most valuable screen in the market-B build is not the shelf — it's "share my list": pick twelve objects, get a clean price list image plus a link, paste into the group. The app becomes the tool that makes a seller look professional in a chat window, and every message it produces carries a link back.
This is also the growth engine and the reason the acquisition cost can be near zero. It is the same received-share screen v3 already needs — no account, no install, opens in a browser.

04 · SACHET PRICING — WHY THE SHAPE OF THE PRICE MATTERS MORE THAN THE PRICE
Shampoo in these markets sells in single-use sachets, not bottles — not because a bottle is bad value, but because the bottle asks for money the buyer doesn't have today. Software follows the same rule: streaming services in India sell weekly plans, mobile data sells in daily packs, and both outperform the monthly equivalent at higher effective cost per day.
The practical consequence: offer the smallest interval Play will let you sell, price it under the cost of a snack, and let people lapse without friction. Churn is not a failure mode here — it's the product working as intended, and they come back the week they have stock to move.

07 · THE COST FLOOR — THE PART THAT ACTUALLY DECIDES IT
At roughly €0.10 of annual revenue per install, any per-user recurring cost is fatal. A service costing €0.02 per user per month is €0.24 a year — more than twice what the average user brings in. This is why the market-B build has to be more disciplined than the market-A one, not less.
