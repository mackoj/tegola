What it fixes

Five things every collector
has said out loud

If you've said one of these, this app was built for you. If you've said none of them, tell us — that's useful too.

"I have a spreadsheet. I haven't opened it since… honestly, years."
Adding takes ten seconds, so it actually gets done

Photo first. The app proposes the pressing, the edition, the year; you confirm. The type already made the decisions your spreadsheet columns never did.

"Who did I lend that to? It's been… a year? Two?"
A lending ledger both sides confirm

Lend with a due date. They confirm they have it; they confirm it came back. Everyone you lend to gets a page and a track record.

"My dad's records are in a crate. I don't even know what's in there."
You don't need to be a collector to find out

No grading scale to learn, no account. Photograph the crate one sleeve at a time and the app tells you what he kept. The history starts with him.

"Every collecting app turns into eBay within a week."
A catalogue first. Not a shop.

No listings, no prices pushed at you, no server copy of your shelf. Your catalogue lives in your iCloud; photos leave the phone only when you ask for a lookup.

"It guessed the wrong pressing and wouldn't let me fix it."
It proposes. You decide. Every time.

Every guess waits in a review queue, marked unconfirmed, until you agree. Real grading scales — CGC, Sheldon, Goldmine — not a free-text box.

Four questions

It answers four questions
about everything you own

01
What is it
Photo first, barcode second, typing last. The catalogue proposes the pressing, the edition, the year — you confirm it.
02
Where is it
Shelf, crate, cellar rack, your brother's flat. Locations are real places, not a text field you stop filling in.
03
Who has it
A lending ledger with a due date on every loan. Both sides confirm the hand-off and the return.
04
What happened to it
Lent, returned scratched, serviced, regraded, opened. Events add to the object's story instead of overwriting it.
Built hobby by hobby

Your hobby's own words.
One app's manners.

A record has a pressing and a Goldmine grade. A bottle has a vintage and a drinking window. A 1989 computer has a board revision and a battery that should have been replaced twenty years ago. Each type knows its own facts — and every type is added, lent, found and confirmed exactly the same way. Learn it on records, and you already know it on bottles.

From one collector to another

I collect records. Around three hundred, mostly seventies pressings, nothing that would impress you. For fifteen years I kept them in a spreadsheet, and for about twelve of those years the spreadsheet was wrong.

Two things finally broke me. I lent a first pressing to a friend and, two years later, neither of us could remember whether it had come back. And every app I tried instead turned out to be a shop — it wanted a price on everything, and it wanted my shelf on its server.

So I'm building the thing I wanted. It's called CLCTN. It's built with collectors: every type is written with the people who collect that thing. If you own things on purpose, I'd like you to look at it and tell me what I got wrong.

— Jérôme, one collector to another

Questions people ask
When can I actually use it?

The catalogue and the lending ledger go to a small beta first, with import right behind. Value tracking, wishlists and anything involving money come later, on purpose. Leave an email above and you'll hear when the beta is real.

Is there a subscription?

No. One payment unlocks the app permanently, including every type and feature added later. If we ever build something that costs us money every month to run, it will be its own optional thing — never a rent on what you already bought.

Do I need an account?

No. Cataloguing works with no sign-up and no network, and syncs across your Apple devices through your own iCloud. We never hold a copy of your shelf.

My hobby isn't one of the sixteen.

Build it. Borrow the nearest type and edit it, or declare your own fields from scratch, then propose it. Types collectors vote up get built properly — with a real grading scale — and your objects migrate into the official version with every value intact.

Android? Web?

iPhone first, then iPad and Mac as one purchase. An Android version is planned for later, as a separate build. There is no web version, because your catalogue doesn't live on a server.

What if you stop working on it?

Your data is in your iCloud and exports to CSV any day you like, with each type's schema attached. The app keeps working without a server because there isn't one. That's the honest answer, and it's also the design.
