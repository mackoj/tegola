01 · THE REFRAME THAT ANSWERS THE QUESTION
Android is not a second copy of your iOS business. Android is market B — the one section 04 of the plan already told you to build for, and then deferred because you had no way to reach it.
The plan already sorts the world into three market types: A pays for tools and hates ads, B is huge and price-sensitive and fine with ads, C finishes every deal off-platform. Look at where each one lives by device and the whole question resolves itself.
So "does the business plan work on Android" is the wrong question. The right one: does the market-B profile you already designed actually earn? Android is just the only delivery mechanism for it. And the honest answer to that is in section 05 — it earns, but not much, and never per-user.

06 · THE REAL ARGUMENT FOR ANDROID ISN'T CONSUMER REVENUE
v3 · INSTITUTIONS · THE UNDERRATED CASE
A recyclerie doesn't get to choose what phones its volunteers own.
The plan's v3 needs staff devices — the shared shelf, roles, the lend handshake, all on a phone on a shift. In France, roughly two of every three phones in that room run Android. An iOS-only app doesn't sell a smaller institution tier; in most organisations it sells zero, because the coordinator can't run a rota on a device half the volunteers don't have.
The member side is already a link with no account and no install, so it costs nothing today. It's the staff side that is quietly iOS-gated — and v3 was named the best possible plan B.
Test it for free before writing any Kotlin: ask the recyclerie contact what the volunteers on shift carry. That is question seven, and it costs one line in an email you're already sending.
