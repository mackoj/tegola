16Wishlists, and the index underneath them

A wishlist is a collection of objects you don't own — same types, same fields, one flag. That one flag buys three things at once: a shareable hunt list, a reason for a for-sale search index, and the shape of lending from someone else's shelf. Not-yours reads as a dashed edge and no drop shadow everywhere it appears — the cheapest possible rule, and it holds from a 44px thumb to a full row.

16aThe wishlist itself. Priority and a price cap per object, and the one line that matters: three of twelve are for sale right now. Nothing here touches value, insurance or stats.
16bShared, seen by someone else — matched against their shelf. They learn what you want; they learn nothing about what you have. Owned rows get actions, the rest stay dashed and inert.
16cSearching what people will sell. The infra note is the point: the index holds offers, not shelves — an object enters when its owner marks it available and leaves when they don't.
16dThe hit. Fires only on print, grade and cap together — near misses stay silent, or the alert becomes noise and gets switched off in a week.
16eThe library idea, sketched not built: a reservation is a queued lend, so it reuses the handshake and adds only a waiting line. Marked not built on the screen itself.

Two things I'd want your call on. First: does a wishlist live inside the Shelf tab as a collection with a flag, or is it its own destination? I've built it as a collection, which keeps the tab bar at five. Second: 16c means publishing a for-sale index — that's the first piece of shared infrastructure in the app, and it's hard to take back once people are searching it.

5Where does "Look it up" live?
