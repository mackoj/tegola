Assume the app is built. This page is every field of the App Store product page, filled in as it would ship — name, subtitle, ten screenshots, description, keywords, price, privacy — with the character limit next to each and the reason for each choice underneath.

The listing is v1: a catalogue and a lending ledger, no money anywhere. Every phone is the live prototype running with selling off. Counters are live — edit any field and the number updates. Dashed chips are the alternatives I didn't pick; argue for them.

02 · The vitrine
Ten screenshot slots,
nine used

Screenshots are the page. Most people never expand the description, and the first three are visible without scrolling — so those three carry the whole pitch: the shelf, one object, adding one. Then the four things nobody else does. Then trust.

Composition rule: one headline per shot, never more than seven words, in the app's own type on the app's own paper. No feature bullets on the image, no arrows, no device colour choices — the phone frame is ink, like everything else. The tenth slot stays empty on purpose; a listing that ends on "trust" ends stronger than one that ends on "and also".

Notes for App Review
the reviewer reads this, nobody else
No account or sign-in exists; all data is stored in the user's private iCloud database. The camera is used to add objects. The "Look it up" toggle in the viewfinder sends a single frame to our identification service only when enabled; it is off by default and the sent image is not retained. To test lending without a second device: add any object → "Lend to…" → pick "Demo contact". The confirmation arrives after 10 seconds in review builds. The in-app purchase "CLCTN Complete" is a non-consumable; sandbox purchase unlocks a second collection immediately. Share links (clctn.app/s/…) open a web view of one object and require nothing installed.

08 · Where I want your eye
Three open questions
Q1 · THE NAME
Is "CLCTN" a brand or a handicap?
Five consonants. Unpronounceable on first sight, unsearchable until known, and it's why the name field spends 22 characters explaining itself. My answer: keep it, because it looks like a catalogue number, and once you've heard it said out loud you never forget how it's spelled. I'm aware that's the argument every bad name makes.
Q2 · SCREENSHOT ONE
Shelf first, or object first?
The shelf shows breadth and looks like a product. The object shows the 3D sleeve, which is the only thing on the page that makes people stop scrolling. My answer: shelf, because it answers "what is this" and the object answers "why is this nice" — and the order of those two questions matters more than which one is prettier. You've watched more people scroll store pages than I have.
Q3 · THE FIFTY-OBJECT LINE
Should the free tier's ceiling be on the store page at all?
Naming the limit is honest and pre-empts the one-star "bait" review. It also tells the hardcore collector, before he's downloaded, that the free app isn't for him. My answer: name it, in the last section, in plain numbers — and let the founding price directly above it do the reassuring.
