Competition · FR · DE · GB · US · August 2026
They sell an
answer. You sell
a shelf.

Forty rankings across four storefronts, picked on proximity of positioning. Most of these apps do the same thing: point the camera at an object, get a number, close the app. A few are price references with a decade of data behind them. Not one is a place where you keep what you own. That gap is the whole strategy — and the thing most likely to be thrown away by accident.

The four markets are not one fight, and reading them as one is the most expensive mistake available here. France and Germany are open fields. The UK is contested. The US is locked. One publisher leads all four.

A scanner app has no reason to be opened twice. CLCTN has no reason to be opened once — until the shelf exists. Everything below is about surviving that asymmetry.

Caveat carried over from the source research: this segment is flooded with 2026 apps built on the same template, most with 0–10 ratings. Ranks 2–6 reflect positioning overlap, not measurable market share. Only AntiqSnap, PriceCharting and Discogs have verifiable traction.

01 · the map
Three markets wear
the same camera icon

Sorting the field by the job it's hired for, rather than by the feature it screenshots, splits it cleanly. Only one of these three is CLCTN's actual market, and it's the emptiest.

GROUP A · THE BULK OF ALL FOUR LISTS
The appraisers
Job: "I'm standing in a flea market holding a thing. Is it worth buying?" Answered in nine seconds, by an LLM, with a confidence it hasn't earned.
Who: AntiqSnap everywhere, plus ThriftAI, Curio, Zophi, Relic, Collectibles.com, Sprice-it, Price Snap, WhatsWorth, Value Scout, Valora, Vintiq, Loot AI.
Weak spot: nothing accumulates. Subscription revenue on an app with no second session is a churn machine, which is why they're all buying installs on the same keywords.
Not your competitor. Your top of funnel.
GROUP B · THE INCUMBENTS
The references
Job: "What is the accepted market price of this exact pressing / this exact cart?" Answered from real closed sales, accumulated over fifteen years.
Who: Discogs (9,777 FR · 12,269 DE · 23,167 GB · 67,056 US), PriceCharting, CollX, Collectr — and in Britain, CeX, which isn't an app company at all.
Weak spot: one vertical each, and a collection UI that is a database form with a logo on it. Neither will ever hold your watches next to your comics.
Your real competitor — and your best supplier.
GROUP C · NOBODY, ANYWHERE
The registers
Job: "This is mine. I want to see it, lend it, insure it, want the next one, and one day sell it to someone who deserves it."
Who: CLCTN. Collectr and PokéCardex do this for one card game. Nobody does it across types, and nobody does it beautifully.
Weak spot: it demands effort before it gives pleasure. That is the single hardest problem in the product, and it is not a competitive problem — it's an onboarding one.
Empty category. Yours to define or lose.

FOUR ADVANTAGES THAT ARE REAL
Ownership is the primitive. Every competitor treats the object as a question to be answered and discarded. You treat it as a thing that is kept, moves, gets lent, gets insured, gets passed on. That is a fundamentally stickier relationship and none of them can retrofit it.
One shelf, many types. Discogs cannot add watches. PriceCharting cannot add comics. A vertical's specialisation is exactly what stops it widening — and collectors are almost never monogamous.
No account, no server, no rent. The data lives in the user's iCloud. It is a genuine trust argument in a category where every rival is a subscription pointed at a database they control.
It's beautiful, in a segment of clones — in France. Eight of the ten FR apps are the same template, and craft is visible in three screenshots. This advantage does not cross the Atlantic: Curio sits at 4.82★ and PriceCharting at 4.83★. Treat craft as a French differentiator and a US entry requirement.
FIVE WAYS YOU LOSE
You invent a number. The day your estimate disagrees with PriceCharting on a cart a user knows the price of, your credibility on all fourteen types goes with it. Estimation is the highest-risk surface in the product.
You ask for work before you give pleasure. A scanner pays out in nine seconds. Your shelf pays out at object thirty. If the first session doesn't produce something worth showing someone, nothing else on this page matters.
Nobody searches for what you are. "Scan value flea market" is a high-intent query with volume. "Collection manager" is not. Your discovery problem is real and the appraisers already own the good keywords.
Vinyl walks to Discogs. Your flagship type has an incumbent with the collection, the want list and the marketplace already built — 9,777 ratings in France, 67,056 in the US. Leading with records is leading with your hardest fight.
The number one is already answering your premise. Next Vision runs AntiqSnap plus VinylSnap plus ToyWorth: your fourteen types, split across fourteen apps. Their bet is that collectors will install one app per hobby. Yours is that they won't. That is the actual argument, and it hasn't been settled.
07 · the sixth meter
The day you take a cut of
sales, you change opponents

The five meters in the revenue model never touch a transaction — that's why none of the forty apps above competes with them. A take-rate on sales is a different business with a different competitive set, and the apps that beat you there aren't on any of these four tables. Worth reading before it gets built, not after.

A marketplace is not a feature, it's a liquidity problem. You do not win one by building a better sell screen — you win it by being the place buyers already are. On vinyl, games and cards in your four markets, they already are somewhere else.
Who takes the cut today
Discogs Marketplace. Records, in all three European markets, with the catalogue, the want lists, the seller reputations and the buyers. The single hardest liquidity pool on your list.
eBay. Everything, everywhere, with the deepest buyer base and the highest fees. The default when an object has no natural home.
CollX and Collectr. Cards, with trading and selling already inside the register — the exact shape you'd be building, done first, on the vertical you're least able to take.
CeX. Not a marketplace at all: a firm price, instantly, in a shop. On a £6 game it wins outright, because the seller's real cost is time.
Vinted, Leboncoin, Mercari. Generalist resale with enormous casual supply. They don't estimate anything — they just already have the buyer.
What you'd actually have that they don't
Supply that was catalogued before it was listed. Condition, provenance, purchase date and photos already recorded, by someone who owned the thing on purpose. Every marketplace above is full of listings written in ninety seconds by someone who wants rid of it.
The mixed basket. Nobody sells a record, a watch and three comics to the same buyer. Your index is the only one where a collector's whole disposal is one act instead of four accounts.
Wishlist matching against real intent. D30 already fires on the full match — print, grade, price cap. That is a buyer signal no marketplace has, because nobody tells eBay precisely what they want and waits.
No shelf is ever published. D29 indexes offers, not collections. That's a privacy claim no marketplace can make, and it matters more to a collector than to a flipper.
THE VERSION THAT WORKS
Charge for the introduction, not the escrow.
A fee on a completed introduction between two people who already have the app needs no payment rail, no buyer protection, no dispute team, no fraud surface and — crucially — no liquidity threshold. It earns from the very first match, where a marketplace earns nothing until it has thousands of listings. It also composes with the insurance referral you already have: same mechanic, same trust, same absence of infrastructure. Escalate to held payments only if the offer index proves real depth in one country and one type.
THREE CONDITIONS BEFORE IT'S WORTH BUILDING
Density in one country, one type. A hundred sellers spread across four markets and fourteen types is not a market, it's a graveyard of unanswered offers. Pick the type where your users cluster and gate the rest.
A floor on object value. CeX proves that below roughly €30 the seller wants cash now, not a buyer. Anything cheap will never transact through you — design the flag to admit that instead of pretending.
Sourced pricing already shipped. Nobody accepts a fee on a sale priced by a number you invented. Move 01 isn't just credibility for the catalogue — it's the precondition for ever earning on a transaction.

Commercial terms for the marketplaces named above change often and vary by category and country — confirm current rates before any of this reaches the business plan.

08 · what to focus on
Five moves, in this order

Each one turns a weakness above into a position none of the forty apps can copy without becoming a different app.

01
Cite the price. Never invent it.
BEFORE LAUNCH
THE MOVE
Every figure in the app carries its source and its date: "Discogs median, 14 sales, last 90 days." Where there's no source, show a range or show nothing. Turn PriceCharting and Discogs from rivals into cited suppliers — the market-data subscription meter is already built for exactly this.
WHY IT WINS
The eight appraisers cannot do this — attribution would expose that their number came from a model. Sourced pricing is the one credibility gap in the segment, and it's free for you to take.
Store copy that follows from it: "We don't guess what it's worth. We show you who paid what."
02
Make the first session end in something showable
BEFORE LAUNCH
THE MOVE
Ten objects, five minutes, and the reward is a shelf worth screenshotting — the 3D sleeve, the shared link, the value strip. Scan-a-row-of-spines, if it can be made to work, beats every other onboarding idea you have. The competitor's payoff is nine seconds; yours has to arrive before object ten, not object thirty.
WHY IT WINS
This is where you'll actually die if you die. It's also your only organic acquisition channel: a shared shelf is a screenshot in a collector Discord, and nothing any of the ten produces is worth sharing.
Metric to instrument now: share of first sessions reaching 10 objects, and share producing a shared link within 7 days.
03
Take the ground nobody else can stand on: the mixed shelf
LAUNCH POSITIONING · FR FIRST
THE MOVE
Lead the store page with the collector who has records and games and a watch — never with one type. Don't launch vinyl-first into Discogs' teeth; lead with the type mix and let vinyl be one shelf among several. Insurance export is the killer proof of the mixed shelf: one document, everything you own.
WHY IT WINS
A vertical cannot follow you here without betraying the audience that made it. Discogs adding watches would be absurd; you adding watches is the premise.
Category line to own: not "the app that prices your stuff" — "the register of everything you own on purpose."
04
Let the appraisers feed you
POST-LAUNCH
THE MOVE
Their user is a person who scans objects and gets nothing to keep. Free tier = 100 objects and identification; the graduation moment is "you've now scanned forty things, here is what they look like together." Rank against their keywords with a free identify, convert on the shelf.
WHY IT WINS
Their weekly subscriptions churn hard because nothing accumulates. A one-time €29.99 aimed at their lapsed users is a very easy argument, and the founding-price ladder gives you the number to make it with.
Only after moves 01–03 land. Chasing their keywords before the shelf is good just buys you their churn.
05
Go to Germany second, and the United States last
MARKET SEQUENCE
THE MOVE
France to launch and learn. Germany next: zero frontal rivals above 400 ratings, the segment leader at its worst rating of four markets, and your closest analogue at fifty. Then the UK, where the English copy is already written. The US only once the register story is provable — and never on scan-and-value, which arrives eleventh.
WHY IT WINS
ThriftAI has 20,238 ratings in the US and 50 in Germany. Traction does not travel in this segment, which means the crowded market cannot fund an assault on the empty one — and the empty one is next door.
German localisation is the cheapest strategic purchase on this page. Budget it for launch + 3 months, not for "later".
09 · the discipline
Six things not to do,
however tempting
Don't put an AI valuation on the hero screen
It files you under "another 2026 scanner", puts you in a ring with eight clones and two real datasets, and stakes your credibility on the one number you can't verify. Value belongs deep in the object page, sourced, secondary to the shelf.
Don't chase the brocante keyword
Price Snap, Scanner Brocante and Vintiq are already there, all bidding, none retaining. It's the wrong user anyway: a flipper doesn't keep things, and everything you've built assumes keeping.
Don't subscription-ise the catalogue
The one-time unlock isn't just a pricing preference — it's the loudest difference between you and every app on the list. Renting someone their own shelf back would delete the trust argument that makes the mixed shelf worth building.
Don't out-catalogue Discogs
Fifteen years and sixty-seven thousand US ratings say you won't win depth on records — and CollX says the same about trading cards. Win on the objects they'll never hold together, and let them be the sources that make those pages trustworthy.
Don't open four markets at once
Four store pages, four keyword sets, four support languages and four competitive sets, on one product with no ratings anywhere. Traction demonstrably doesn't travel in this segment — ThriftAI is 20,238 in the US and 50 in Germany. Win one, then port.
Don't build escrow to earn on sales
Held payments, buyer protection and disputes are a company, not a feature — and they only pay once you have liquidity you won't have for years. Take the fee on the introduction. It earns on the first match and costs you nothing to run.
Across four markets, every one of these apps answers "what is this worth?". Nobody is answering "what do I have, and what happens to it?" Stay on the second question, cite the answers to the first, and the crowded half of the market becomes your funnel instead of your fight.
