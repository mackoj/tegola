CLCTN · PLATFORM WATCH · IPHONE DUO · ASSESSED 19 SEP 2026
The phone folds. The app mostly shouldn't care.
Apple announced the iPhone Duo on 9 September: a book-style foldable with a 5.4″ outer display and a 7.6″ inner one, from $1,999, shipping 23 October. The right response for CLCTN is one engineering constraint and one idea parked for later — not a redesign, and not a single Duo-specific screen.
The reason this page exists rather than a Slack message: the constraint is cheap today and expensive in eighteen months. Fixed-width grids and orientation-keyed layout are the kind of debt you only discover when a device you didn't plan for makes them visible — and iPad makes them visible anyway.

02 · WHAT THE HIG ACTUALLY ASKS FOR
Less than you'd expect, and that is the headline. Apple explicitly does not want a custom layout per fold angle — it names the physical positions "poses" and then tells you to ignore them. You design for two size classes and let the system's layout engine do the adapting.
Practically, the work is an audit rather than a build: find the places where our layout is keyed off something that isn't available width, and stop doing that.

04 · THE ONE GENUINELY INTERESTING IDEA
TRIPOD SHELF CAPTURE · PARKED, NOT PLANNED
The phone stands up on its own, and you step back.
The hardest photo in CLCTN is the first one: a whole bookcase, shot straight on, far enough back and steady enough that shelf segmentation has something to work with. Held in one hand at arm's length, that photo is crooked, close, and shot at whatever angle the wall allows.
A half-folded Duo stands on a table by itself, and Apple's camera work puts a live preview on the outer display while the high-resolution rear cameras shoot. So the collector props it up, walks to the shelf, and watches the framing from where they're standing. That is a better shelf photo than a tripod they don't own.
Worth writing down, worth zero engineering now. It pairs with the shelf-segmentation model in On-Device ML Ideas — and if that model is ever built, this is the capture flow it deserves. Both are contingent; neither is a v1 commitment.

05 · THE ACTUAL CHANGE TO MAKE — AND WHY IT ISN'T A DUO COST
One paragraph into the Technical Blueprint, and nothing else. The reason it is worth doing has almost nothing to do with the Duo. "Works at any width" is one rule that buys four platforms — and three of them are ones we plausibly ship to.
Framed as foldable support, this is speculative work for a device nobody owns. Framed as resizability, it is the price of admission for iPad and Mac — both of which are real destinations for a cataloguing app, where a big screen genuinely serves the task. The Duo is the cheapest of the four to support because the other three already forced the work.
